About the DMI

The Distributional Misery Index (DMI) measures economic pressure across income groups by combining inflation and unemployment rates with income-specific expenditure patterns.

DMI(g,r,t) = λ × [α × π(g,r,t) + (1-α) × S(r,t)]

Where: α = 0.5 (equal weight), π = group-weighted inflation, S = unemployment rate, λ = 2.0 (scaling factor)

Why the scaling factor? The traditional Misery Index (Okun, 1970s) is simply Inflation + Unemployment, which produces values in the 10-15 range. With α = 0.5, the weighted average would compress DMI into a 3-8 range, making it harder to compare against the familiar scale.

At the baseline α = 0.5, multiplying by λ = 2.0 is mathematically equivalent to the simple sum (π + S), preserving comparability with the traditional Misery Index. When α is varied in sensitivity analyses, DMI will intentionally diverge from this baseline, reflecting a deliberate reweighting of the two components.

The α + λ formulation separates two design choices: the relative weighting between components (α) and the level calibration (λ), making it easy to explore alternative weightings without losing historical comparability.

Key Insight: Lower-income quintiles often experience higher DMI values due to spending more on necessities (food, housing) which may inflate faster than discretionary items.

Data Sources: BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey (CE), CPI-U, and Current Population Survey (CPS).
